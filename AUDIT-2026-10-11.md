Scope: `src/game/poker/**`, `PokerTable.tsx`, `PokerMatch.tsx`, `pokerLayout.ts`, `HowToPlay.tsx`, `CoachOverlay.tsx`, `CardFaceV4.tsx`, match-result reporting (`src/lib/supabase.ts`, `src/meta/matchHistory.ts`, `goldfish.ts`, `coachPractice.ts`, `supabase/migrations/*poker*`).

How this was verified:
- **Engine fuzz.** A throwaway driver plays 260 matches (2–6 seats, all modes). After every action it checks chip conservation, integer and non-negative stacks, that a `bet` wait always has `betOptions`, that no wait points at an all-in seat, and that no `start` wait arrives while a hand is live. **Result: no crash, no stall, no chip leak.** The engine core is sound. - **Real-table runs.** Playwright (Chromium at `/opt/pw-browsers/chromium`) drives `table-preview.html` on the Vite dev server. Scripts are in the scratchpad `probe/` directory (`pw-dbl.mts`, `pw-pass.mts`, `pw-spot.mts`, `pw-range.mts`, …).

Severity scale: **S1** loses a hand or match for the player, or stalls the table · **S2** wrong result, rule violation or money mismatch · **S3** cosmetic, copy or minor.

### Buttons that break the match

#### A1 · S1 · Hovering the cast spotlight opens a full-screen inspector that kills the hotkeys and covers every action button. A pointer that is simply resting there is enough to trigger it.
`src/components/PokerTable.tsx:1756` (`<div onMouseEnter={() => onInspect(def)}>` in `Spotlight`), together with `:457` (keyboard effect bails while `inspect` is set) and `:462` (it also bails while any `aria-modal` dialog is up).

**Scenario.** The spotlight is a 'standard' card face centred on the felt, which is exactly where a player's pointer tends to sit. When a CPU casts, the spotlight mounts under the pointer and Chromium fires `mouseenter` even though the pointer never moved. `setInspect(def)` then mounts `Card3DInspector`, a `fixed inset-0 z-50` backdrop with `aria-modal`.
- The inspector does **not** close when the spotlight times out.
- F, C and R do nothing.
- FOLD, CALL and RAISE sit under the backdrop, so the first click only closes the inspector.
- Response windows run their 10 s auto-pass and the turn timer keeps counting toward the auto check/fold. The player can lose the decision without ever seeing it.
- After a backdrop click while the spotlight is still up, the pointer is over the spotlight again and the inspector re-opens.

**Verified.** In `probe/pw-spot.mts` (6 seats, Deep, seed 3, BOTS 2×) the mouse is parked at (640, 330) and never moved. At 3.8 s the spotlight appears and `Inspecting Ruin-Walker Overseer` opens on its own. Three response windows then auto-passed under it. When `data-waiting=bet:0`, pressing `C` left the table at `bet:0`, and `elementFromPoint` at the centre of the FOLD button returned the inspector dialog.

**Fix.** Never open a modal on hover.
- Change the handler to a click: `<button onClick={() => onInspect(def)} title="Click to inspect">`.
- If a hover preview is wanted, render a non-modal `pointer-events-none` enlargement inside the Spotlight, without `aria-modal`, that the hotkey effect does not check.
- Also clear `inspect` when the spotlight it came from goes away: track `inspectFrom: 'spot' | 'user'` and in the `setSpot(null)` timeout call `setInspect(i => (inspectFromRef.current === 'spot' ? null : i))`.

#### A2 · S1 · Double-clicking PASS in a response window folds (or calls) the hand that follows
`PokerTable.tsx:1337-1343` (PASS) and `:1358-1387` (FOLD/CALL row). `humanAct` (`:338`) has no per-decision guard.

**Scenario.** A CPU raises or shoves and the human holds a castable Quick or Ambush card, so a raise window opens with a full-width yellow PASS button at the bottom of the actions column (desktop y = 668–704). The first click passes. React re-renders synchronously and the human is now to act, facing the bet. When there is no raise option (facing an all-in, so no slider row), the FOLD/CALL row is bottom-aligned in the same column and lands under the pointer. The second click of the double-click then hits **FOLD** on the left half of the old PASS button, or **CALL** of the all-in on the right half.

**Verified.** In `probe/pw-pass.mts` (3 seats, Deep, seed 5, 1280×720), 12 PASS double-clicks. One of them logged:
```
Ethereal Sea Witch bets 522 — ALL IN.
You folds.
Ethereal Sea Witch wins 1922 uncontested.
```
That is a 1,922-chip pot thrown away by one double-click. Measured boxes: PASS x 850–1260, y 668–704. FOLD with a slider row present is x ≈ 920–1045, y 629–684, so it only just misses. Without the slider row the FOLD/CALL row drops into PASS's band.

**Fix.** Make every human control idempotent per decision.
- Keep a decision token, e.g. `decisionKey = `${match.handNo}:${match.hand?.street}:${match.log.length}``, captured when the controls render. In `humanAct`, drop the action when `decisionKeyRef.current !== keyAtRender`.
- Simpler: ignore any human action within ~350 ms of the previous successful one. Add `lastActAt = useRef(0)`, and in `humanAct` do `if (Date.now() - lastActAt.current < 350) return; … on success lastActAt.current = Date.now()`.
- Also hold the action row inert (`pointer-events:none`) for ~300 ms after it first mounts.

#### A3 · S1 · Double-clicking CHECK or CALL acts on two streets
Same root cause as A2: `PokerTable.tsx:1365-1378` and `humanAct :338`.

**Scenario.** Heads-up with the human in the big blind: the CPU limps and the human double-clicks CHECK. The first click closes pre-flop, the flop is dealt, and the human is first to act after the flop. The CHECK button re-renders in the same spot and the second click checks the flop too. In multi-way pots the same thing happens whenever the human closes a street and opens the next one (the small blind or big blind when the seats before them folded). It also happens with the C hotkey when it is tapped twice. `e.repeat` only filters auto-repeat, not two separate presses.

**Verified.** `probe/pw-dbl.mts` (2 seats, Quick, seed 7) ran one `dblclick()` on CHECK and the log showed both actions:
```
Kuro, the Unseen calls 8.
You checks.
FLOP: 9♥ 5♦ 4♦
You checks.
```
This reproduced on two different hands.

**Fix.** Same guard as A2. In the hotkey handler also require that the key-down is the first since the decision started (`if (decisionKeyRef.current !== keyAtBind) return`).

#### A4 · S2 · Opening a power in a response window turns off the auto-pass, so an idle player can hold up the table forever
`PokerTable.tsx:388-401`: `composing = !!castFlow`, and the effect returns early while `composing`. `HumanPanel` also shows `WINDOW_AUTOPASS_MS` frozen (`:862-868`).

**Scenario.** A CPU casts or raises, and the human opens one of their Quick or Ambush cards from the window (or clicks one and goes to make coffee). The auto-pass is suspended for as long as the dialog stays open. No other timer covers a window (the turn timer only runs on `myTurn`), so every seat waits indefinitely. On the player's own turn the same dialog is still bounded by the turn timer, so the two states behave inconsistently.

**Verified.** `probe/pw-timers.mts` (3 seats, Deep, seed 5) opened the cast dialog in `window:0` and waited 25 s, which is 2.5× the auto-pass. Afterwards the table was still `data-waiting=window:0`, the dialog was still open, and no countdown was on screen.

**Fix.** Pause the countdown instead of disabling it.
- Keep `windowDeadline = useRef(0)`: set it to `Date.now() + WINDOW_AUTOPASS_MS` when the window opens, add the time spent composing when the dialog closes, and cap the composing extension, e.g. one extra `TURN_TIMER_MS`.
- Or keep a hard ceiling: in the composing branch, schedule `setTimeout(() => { setCastFlow(null); humanAct({type:'pass', seat:HUMAN}); }, TURN_TIMER_MS)`.
- Show the remaining time inside `CastDialog` as well.

#### A5 · S2 · The BOTS speed toggle (and any other driver-effect re-run) restarts the human's turn timer and the think time charged to the clock
`PokerTable.tsx:347-374`: the driver effect depends on `[match, mult, speedMult, busted, over, dispatch]`, and its "human's input" branch runs `promptAt.current = Date.now(); setPromptStart(...)`.

**Scenario.** On their turn, the player clicks `BOTS 1× → 2× → INSTANT → 1×`. Each click re-runs the effect and resets `promptAt`, so:
1. The 30 s soft timer and the time bank start over. The player can stall indefinitely and the auto check/fold never fires.
2. `humanAct` charges `dt = Date.now() - promptAt`, so the blind clock is under-charged. Cycling the toggle before each action freezes the blinds and the time cap, which defeats the design note "the blinds rise on the clock, not on how fast you click".

**Verified.** `probe/pw-timers.mts`: at the human's turn the timer read **22s**. After three speed clicks back to the same speed it read **30s**.

**Fix.** Start the think clock once per decision, not once per effect run. Key it on the decision itself:
```ts
const decisionKey = waiting.kind === 'bet' || waiting.kind === 'choice' ? `${waiting.kind}:${waiting.seat}:${match.handNo}:${match.hand?.street}:${match.hand?.casts.length}` : waiting.kind === 'window' ? `w:${waiting.castId}:${match.log.length}` : '';
const lastKey = useRef('');
// in the human branch:
if (lastKey.current !== decisionKey) { lastKey.current = decisionKey; promptAt.current = Date.now(); setPromptStart(promptAt.current); }
```
Alternatively, move the `promptAt` update into its own effect that depends only on `[myTurn, myWindow, myChoice, match.handNo, match.hand?.street]`.

#### A6 · S2 · The raise slider cannot reach an odd pot-limit maximum or all-in; dragging it fully right leaves a chip behind
`PokerTable.tsx:1391-1400` (`<input type="range" min={opts.minRaiseTo} max={opts.maxRaiseTo} step={2}>`). The `potRaise` presets (`:1154-1160`) also round to even.

**Scenario.** Every amount is now a whole chip, and from blind level 3 the small blind is odd (bb 50/sb 25, then 125, …), so pots and stacks go odd. In Standard at level 3, UTG faces min 100 and max **175**. A range with `step=2` from 100 snaps to 100, 102, … 174, so the slider stops at **174**. If the stack is the binding limit (an odd stack), a full-right drag shows "RAISE TO x" without "ALL IN" and leaves the player with one chip, which the next blind then takes. The coach text claims the slider "stops at the size of the pot". Only the POT preset (clamped by `Math.min`) reaches the maximum. When `minRaiseTo` is odd, the slider's own minimum still works, but every value it offers is odd.

**Verified.**
- Engine (`probe/odd.ts`): Standard, level 3, 3 seats returns `minRaiseTo: 100, maxRaiseTo: 175`.
- Chromium (`probe/pw-range.mts`): `<input type=range min=100 max=175 step=2>`, with the End key and with a click at the far right, gives value **174**.

**Fix.** Use `step={1}`, since chips are whole now. Or keep a coarse step but snap the extremes: in `onChange`, `const v = Number(e.target.value); setRaiseTo(v >= opts.maxRaiseTo - 1 ? opts.maxRaiseTo : v)`. In `potRaise`, drop the `/2*2` rounding (`Math.round(h!.currentBet + (pot + owe) * frac)`).

#### A7 · S3 · Any illegal bot action freezes the table silently (a latent stall with no fallback)
`PokerTable.tsx:367-373`: `const t = window.setTimeout(() => dispatch(action!), delay)`. When `dispatch` returns false, the state does not change, so the effect never re-runs and nothing is ever retried. The bot's error text also shows up as the *human's* red notice (`setNotice(e.message)`).

**Scenario.** Any future bot or engine drift where `botAction` proposes an illegal move (for example a cast whose target becomes illegal after a response) leaves the table waiting on a bot forever. The scripted `runBots` would throw; the UI just hangs.

**Verified.** By code reading. `probe/botfuzz.ts` (300 bot-only matches, 88,861 actions, all skills and seat counts) produced **no** illegal bot action today, so this is defensive.

**Fix.** In the timeout, use `if (!dispatch(action!)) dispatch(w.kind === 'window' ? {type:'pass', seat: bot} : w.kind === 'choice' ? {type:'choose', seat: bot, index: 0} : {type:'fold', seat: bot});`. Do not surface a bot's `IllegalAction` as the human's notice: pass a `quiet` flag to `dispatch`.

### Other bugs

#### Rules and engine

#### A8 · S2 · A seat can answer the same response window twice (Redraw or Exhume cast as a response)
`src/game/poker/engine.ts:1665-1673` (`cast()` response branch) together with `queueChoice :2175-2183` and `choose :2235`.

**Scenario.** A response that opens a choice (Clash of Titans: Quick Redraw; Glowing Sea Spider: Ambush Exhume) pushes the open window onto `h.deferred` **before** `cast()` removes the responder from `p.seats`, because by then `h.pending` is the choice, so the `p.seats.filter(...)` branch is skipped. When the choice is made, `choose()` restores the window with the responder still listed, and they may cast a second response. RULEBOOK §6.4.3 says each seat "may cast **one** response or pass". A bot or human can chain Redraw → choose → second response.

**Verified.** `probe/deferred.ts`: S0 casts, giving a window of [1, 2]. S1 responds with a Quick Redraw, giving choice(1) with `deferred=[{response, seats:[1,2]}]`. After choose the window is again `[1,2]`, `canCast` for S1's second Quick card is `{ok:true}`, and S1 ends with **2** responses to cast #1.

**Fix.** Remove the responder from the window before resolving:
```ts
if (isResponse) {
  const p = h.pending;
  if (p && p.kind !== 'choice') p.seats = p.seats.filter((x) => x !== a.seat);
  const closeAfter = !!p && p.kind !== 'choice' && p.seats.length === 0;
  resolveCast(m, rec);
  if (closeAfter && h.pending === p) closeWindow(m);
  return;
}
```
Also, when `choose()` pops a deferred window whose `seats` is empty, call `closeWindow`. Otherwise `waitingOn` returns `window` with no seats; the UI treats that as the human's input and stalls.

#### A9 · S2 · Fused and Thriving hostile effects keep landing on a seat that has folded
`engine.ts:909-920` (Thriving checks only `inHand(h, u.seat)`, the owner) and `:921-929` (Fuse checks only `inHand(h, cast.seat)`, the caster). Neither checks `rec.target`. `resolveCast :1837` fizzles a cast whose target folded, but these delayed paths skip that check.

**Scenario.** S0 casts Kindle with Fuse 1 (or the Thriving Unit *Light Seahorse*, Kindle) at S1, and S1 folds before the flop. When the flop is dealt, the fuse burns down (or the Unit thrives) and drains a player who is out of the hand into a pot they cannot win. That breaks "it fizzles if … folded", and a folded player should be untouchable. Erode, Needle and Venomous behave the same way.

**Verified.** `probe/fold-target.ts`: Fuse-1 Kindle had the folded S1 go 792 → 760 at the flop ("Test Kindle's fuse burns down. / Kindle drains 32 from S1 into the pot."). The Thriving Kindle Unit drained the folded S1 by a further 48 ("Kindle drains 48 from S1").

**Fix.** In both loops, `if (cast.target !== null && !inHand(h, cast.target)) { cast.status = 'fizzled'; say(m, `${cast.def.name} fizzles — its target folded.`); continue; }`. For Thriving, skip the re-fire but keep the token.

#### A10 · S2 · Veil does not hide the target: the public log, `hostileHit` and the target picker reveal it
`engine.ts:1660` (`h.hostileHit[target] = true` is copied into every view); `view.ts:65-70` (only `c.target` is nulled). The effect logs in `applyEffect` name the target for Kindle `:1963`, Erode `:1969`, Bounty `:1982`, Venomous `:2141`, Lock `:2155` and Needle (through `adjustNerve`'s public "loses N nerve (Needle)"). Only Peek checks `rec.veiled`.

**Scenario.** S3 casts a Veiled Lock at S0. Every other seat's log reads "S0 is Locked — no casting this street." `view.hand.hostileHit` is `[true,false,…]`, and their own cast dialog leaves S0 out of the TARGET list. RULEBOOK §9 Veil says "The target stays hidden until the street ends."

**Verified.** `probe/veil.ts`, 4 seats, observer S1: `cast.target=null` is correctly redacted, but `hostileHit=[true,false,false,false]`, `legalTargets` gives `[2,3]`, and the log names S0 for Kindle, Lock, Needle, Venomous, Bounty and Erode.

**Fix.**
- When `rec.veiled`, log the effect privately to the caster and target (`say(m, text, seat)` and `say(m, text, t)`) and publicly as "… a hidden seat …".
- In `viewFor`, redact `h.hostileHit`, `h.locked`, `h.venom` and `h.bounty` entries that come from veiled casts for other seats.
- Keep the engine's real `hostileHit` for legality, so an observer's illegal pick is rejected with a neutral "No legal target" instead of being filtered in advance.
- Stack and nerve changes from Kindle, Erode and Needle are inherently visible; note that in the RULEBOOK.

#### A11 · S2 · The match seed is shown mid-match, which lets a player reconstruct every hole card and the board
`PokerTable.tsx:1847-1849` (HistoryModal: "Match seed {match.seed}… every shuffle can be verified", reachable during play). `engine.ts:2340` (`replay`). `view.ts:44` keeps `seed`.

**Scenario.** The engine and card pool ship in the client bundle. The setup is `cpuTableSetup({seed, …})` plus the human's deck, and the deck shuffle for each hand depends only on the seed and the public action sequence. Bots' private choice indexes do not move the rng. So a player who opens HAND HISTORY mid-match can replay locally and read every opponent's hole cards and the next board cards. This is a direct edge in a mode that pays credits by placement.

**Verified.** By code reading: the shuffles in `startHand` and `rngOn(m)` advance only on engine actions, and `replay(setup, actions)` is documented as exact.

**Fix.**
- During the match, show only a commitment, e.g. `sha256(seed)`. Show the seed itself on GameOver.
- Strip `seed` from `viewFor` (`v.seed = 0`). Bots do not need it.

#### A12 · S3 · A Tilt Zone Leader ability can cost more nerve than the seat holds
`engine.ts:1740` (the check is `s.nerve < -ab.nerve`) and `:1692` (`adjustNerve` doubles the delta in Tilt Zone, `:429`).

**Scenario.** On a Tilt Zone hand, a seat with 2 nerve uses a "−2 nerve" ability. It passes the check, is charged −4, and is clamped to 0 (tilted). A seat with 3 nerve can use a −3 ability and goes straight to 0. Either the check should use the doubled cost, or the ability cost should be "as printed" and not doubled (RULEBOOK §8.3 lists Leader costs as "as printed"; Tilt Zone "doubles every nerve change").

**Fix.** In `canUseLeader`: `const cost = -ab.nerve * (h.rule.id === 'tiltZone' ? 2 : 1); if (ab.nerve < 0 && s.nerve < cost) return {ok:false, why:`Needs ${cost} nerve`}`. Or exempt Leader costs from the doubling.

#### A13 · S3 · Leader Windfall (Mer-King) skips the "deck has 8+ cards" check that the card version has
`engine.ts:1729-1756` (`canUseLeader` has the Straddle and Exhume checks but not `(kw === 'Windfall' || kw === 'Redraw') && h.deck.length < 8`, compare `canCast :1560`).

**Scenario.** On a thin deck (Double Board plus Rerun plus several Redraws at 6 seats), the Leader Windfall reaches `dealCard`, which falls back to the burn and muck piles and can throw `Error('deck exhausted')`. That is not an `IllegalAction`, so `dispatch` rethrows it inside a `setTimeout` or click handler and the table freezes.

**Verified.** By code reading. It is rare in practice.

**Fix.** Mirror the `canCast` check in `canUseLeader`.

#### A14 · S3 · Roulette hostile casts ignore the one-hostile-per-seat-per-street cap
`engine.ts:1865-1869` (Roulette picks `rec.target` among live seats at landing), together with `:1589-1593` and `:1660` (no target at cast time, so no `hostileHit` is set or checked).

**Scenario.** S1 was already hit by a Kindle this street, and a Roulette Kindle Unit lands on S1 again. That breaks "Each seat can be the target of at most one hostile power per street". Toll is also never charged on a Roulette hit.

**Fix.** At landing, filter `live` by `!h.hostileHit[i] || i === rec.seat`; after picking, set `h.hostileHit[rec.target] = true` and apply Toll.

#### A15 · S3 · A cast can pass `canCast` but have no payable second cost (dead CAST button)
`engine.ts:1404-1409` (`extraOptions` counts every excludable category) against `validateExtra :1377` (at most `MAX_EXCLUSIONS - used` exclusions).

**Scenario.** A short stack with no other powers, both hole cards already blinded this street, and one exclusion already used casts a tier-4 card under the 25% stack cap (needs 2 second costs). `extraOptions` returns 3 (three excludable categories), so `canCast` is ok and the card looks castable. The dialog's `toggle` only allows 1 exclusion, so `ready` never becomes true and CAST stays disabled with no reason given.

**Fix.** In `extraOptions`, count `Math.min(excludableCategories(...).length, MAX_EXCLUSIONS - h.exclusions[seat].length)`.

#### A22 · S2 · A seat whose whole contribution is dead money (Bomb Pot ante all-in, drained to 0 before posting) cannot win even the main pot
`engine.ts:1006-1036` (`buildPots`). Pot levels come only from `committed[i] > 0`, and the dead money (antes, drains, power costs, the jackpot) is added to `pots[0]`, which is eligible only to seats with `committed >= L₁`.

**Scenario.** On a Bomb Pot hand, a 10-chip short stack antes all-in. Its 10 chips go to `dead` and its `committed` stays 0. Another seat bets on the flop, so `pots[0]` becomes `{eligible: [bettors]}`, and the dead money, the short stack's own ante included, is put in a pot the short stack is not eligible for. It holds the best hand, wins nothing, and busts. The same thing happens to any seat Kindled or Taxed to 0 before it has posted. RULEBOOK §2.6 says dead money joins the **main** pot, and the main pot is the one every live seat can win.

**Verified.** `probe/bomb.ts`: 3 seats, Bomb Pot (ante 16), S1 at 10 chips holding A♠A♥, A on the board (trips). The result was `pots=[{amount:74, winners:[2], eligible:[0,2]}]` and S1 finished at 0.

**Fix.** Give the dead money its own bottom layer, eligible to every live seat:
```ts
const liveSeats = [...Array(n).keys()].filter(live);
if (h.dead !== 0 && liveSeats.some((i) => h.committed[i] === 0)) pots.unshift({ amount: 0, eligible: liveSeats });
pots[0].amount += h.dead;
```
Or seed `levels` with 0 whenever a live seat has `committed === 0`.

#### A23 · S3 · Escape in the card inspector opened from the cast dialog closes both, and the half-built cast is lost
`PokerTable.tsx:1486-1494` (`useEscape` adds a raw `window` keydown listener) against `useFocusTrap.ts:136-157` (the inspector's `useEscapeClose` stack). The cast dialog's listener is registered first, so it fires first and calls `setCastFlow(null)`, then the inspector's stack handler closes the inspector.

**Scenario.** In the cast dialog the player clicks the card face to inspect it (`:1588`) and presses Escape to go back. The target, second costs and Feint choices are all discarded. The same happens to `HistoryModal`.

#### A16 · S3 · Conceding uses the stale render-time `match`, so the reported place can differ from the one shown

#### A25 · S3 · Rule edge cases the RULEBOOK leaves open but that should be decided
- **Incomplete all-in raises re-open the betting.** `engine.ts:803` resets `acted` for every seat on any raise, including an all-in that is smaller than a full raise (`size < lastRaise`). Standard pot-limit play, and the "minimum raise" in RULEBOOK §2.5, imply that a short all-in does not let players who already acted re-raise. Fix: `if (size >= h.lastRaise) for (…) h.acted[i] = false;` (otherwise only the seats that still owe chips must act).
- **Stack ties at the clock cap always go to the lower seat index, which is the human (seat 0).** `engine.ts:1278`: `b.stack - a.stack || a.idx - b.idx`. Break ties by stack at the start of the last hand, then by an rng draw, or share the place.
- **Pass forgets the passer.** `engine.ts:2054-2061`: the card a seat passes keeps its `knownTo` list but does not add the passer, so the passer's own view loses the card they just held. Add `if (!card.knownTo.includes(i)) card.knownTo.push(i);`.

#### A20 · S2 · SKIP TO RESULT and INSTANT bots routinely finish a match faster than the server's wall-clock floor, so the reward is lost with no retry
`PokerTable.tsx:549-555` (`skipToEnd` fast-forwards headless and reports at once), `CPU_SPEEDS` INSTANT. On the server, `record_match_placement` (migration `20261009000000…sql`) requires `started_at <= now() - floor` with floor = max(45 s, mode minutes × seats / 6): 6 seats Standard is 12 min, 6 seats Quick 6 min. The client `App.tsx:461-469` stops at the first `status`.

**Scenario.** A player busts in minute 4 of a 6-seat Standard match and clicks SKIP TO RESULT (the UI's suggested action). The result posts immediately, the server answers `too_early`, and the player sees "No reward: the match ended too quickly to count", even for a 2nd-place finish. The ticket is still unredeemed, but the client never retries it, and REMATCH or the next `begin_match` deletes it. The same happens to a heads-up winner on INSTANT bots.

**Fix.** Store the ticket's start time in the client (`beginMatch` resolves at match start). In `onResult`, if `Date.now() - startedAt < floorMs(mode, seats)`, either hold the GameOver "Saving your reward…" until the floor passes and then submit, or submit and, on `too_early`, schedule one retry at the floor. Alternatively, have the server record the placement and release the payout when the floor passes, instead of refusing it.

#### A21 · S2 · Mode and seat count are client-asserted at redemption, not recorded at ticket start as RULEBOOK §13 says
`supabase/migrations/20261009000000_poker_placement_rewards.sql`: `begin_match()` takes no mode or seats, and `record_match_placement(p_match_id, p_place, p_seats, p_mode)` trusts both. RULEBOOK §13 says "The match ticket records mode and seat count at the start".

**Scenario.** A player who has just won a Quick heads-up match (floor 2 min) can claim `p_mode='deep', p_seats=2` once 400 s have passed and receive 150 credits instead of 50. Any finish can be claimed as `seats=2, place=1`. Placement itself is documented as client-asserted; mode is supposed to be pinned at the start.

**Also:** the legacy `record_match_result(p_won boolean, p_match_id uuid)` (latest definition in `20261008000002_match_reward_taper.sql`: `v_min_seconds constant int := 45`, `v_reward := case when p_won then 100 else 40 end`) is still granted to `authenticated`. Any ticket can be redeemed through it 45 s after `begin_match` for 100 credits, which bypasses the placement table, the mode multiplier and the per-mode floors.

**Fix.** `begin_match(p_mode text, p_seats int)` stores both on `match_tickets`. `record_match_placement` ignores the client's mode and seats (or rejects a mismatch) and uses the ticket's values for the multiplier and the floor. The client then passes `setup.mode` and `setup.seats` to `beginMatch()`.

Replays stay exact (the `dt` is in the action log), `engine.ts` needs **no** change, and every mode keeps its structure. In practice it is the "blinds by hand count, cap by hand limit" the request asked for, with the hand count calibrated to today's matches:

**What cannot be turned off without breaking a mode**

- **Blind escalation (T1).** A freezeout has to end. The bots' short-stack shove logic (`bot.ts:478`) and the fixed-chip cost ladder (8–96 chips; powers get relatively cheaper as blinds climb) both assume it. Untimed play changes *what drives* the escalation, not whether it happens.
- **The cap (T2) in Quick 6-seat.** 65 % of Quick 6-seat matches end on the cap on HEAD (75 % on the old scale; lab, n = 20) and are ranked by stack. Removing the cap changes the mode's win condition. It could ship as an explicit "Play to the finish" sub-toggle under OFF; `s3/head/struct-nocap.txt` measures what it costs: +1.2 hands per match and no measurable skill effect (see 3.4, F-2).
- **Server floor (T12) and ticket expiry (T13).** These are anti-farm controls and stay. Untimed play only makes matches *longer* in real time, so it can never trip `too_early`. It can trip the 6 h expiry if a tab is left open. **Fix:** when the human has been idle on an OFF table for 5 h, show "This match's reward expires in 60 min".

**Rewards and anti-farm**
- Rewards depend only on place (`rewards.ts:24`; migration), so no timer setting changes what a place pays. OFF lowers credits per real minute and is never a farming lever.
- The one interaction that matters is TM-1 (fast play under the floor). Ship the deferred submit together with this setting.
- Untimed play gives a human unlimited think time against bots. That is acceptable in PvE. If PvP tables ever ship, they must force STANDARD.

### 3.2 CPU solvers (`src/game/poker/bot.ts`)

**What the bot does.** It estimates equity by Monte Carlo (220 trials, 132 pre-flop, `constants.ts:203`) against its "contestants" (`bot.ts:68-74`), with a range floor from public betting (`rangeFloors`, `:138-167`). Equity gets uniform noise of ±0.225·(1−skill) (`:176`) and a pressure discount when facing a bet (`:457-458`). It raises above a `strong` threshold (`:463-471`), stab-bluffs only when checked to (`:491-499`), and calls on raw pot odds plus a margin (`:505-506`). Short stacks (< 10 BB) shove or fold (`:478`). Powers are cast when `value·(0.6+powerUse) − costBB − 0.5 > 0` (`:370`), and the bot only *looks* at its powers on 20–50 % of turns (`:548`) and at its Leader on 50 % (`:550`).

#### Findings

- **B-1 · P1 · Sizing tell.** `bot.ts:483-486` vs `:498`. Value raises use `big = 0.6–1.0` or `small = 0.35–0.6 + 0.25·skill` (0.575–0.875 at Hard). Bluffs use `0.4–0.7`. So below 0.575 pot a Hard bot is *only* bluffing, and a pot-sized raise is a bluff 7 % of the time (measured above on HEAD; the 2-chip rounding at `bot.ts:474` does not blur it). **Fix:** draw bluff and value sizes from one distribution per street. For example `frac = pick([0.5, 0.75, 1.0], weights by street)`, applied to both, with polarised hands (bluffs and the nuts) taking the large size at `skill ≥ 0.7`.
- **B-2 · P1 · A bot raise facing a bet is always value.** The bluff branch requires `o.canCheck` (`:493`), and a raise facing a bet needs `eq > strong + 0.15` (`:469`). No check-raise bluffs, no semi-bluffs, no 3-bet bluffs, so a human can fold everything but the nuts to a bot re-raise. **Fix:** a semi-bluff branch for draws: when facing a bet with `outs ≥ 8` (flush or open-ended draw, cheap to detect from the visible board), raise at `p.bluff·skill·0.6`. Also add a pre-flop 3-bet bluff at `0.04 + 0.06·skill` with suited aces and broadways in late position.
- **B-3 · P1 · No position awareness.** Nothing in `bot.ts` reads `h.button` or the order of action, yet Reverse Order and Straddle Night exist precisely to move position. **Fix:** `posAdj = (seatsLeftToAct === 0 ? −0.04 : +0.02·seatsLeftToAct)` added to `strong` and to the pre-flop "complete the blind" threshold `:503`.
- **B-4 · P1 · Blind to the clock and the cap.** No reference to `clockMs`/`capMs` in `bot.ts`, but 28–65 % of Quick 6-seat matches end on the cap on HEAD (33–75 % old scale) and are ranked by stack (`engine.ts:1278`). Near the cap a chip leader should fold its way in and a short stack must gamble. Human exploit: as chip leader, sit out the last level; bots keep calling off. **Fix:** pass `handsToCap ≈ (capMs − clockMs) / avgHandMs` in the read. If the bot is top-3 by stack and `handsToCap ≤ 2`, `tight += 0.2`; if it is outside the top half and `handsToCap ≤ 2`, use the shove branch at `fair − 0.05`.
- **B-5 · P2 · Plays chip-EV, not placement.** Rewards pay by place (6-seat factors 1/.6/.35/.15/.05/0), but calling decisions ignore survival. **Fix:** when ≤ 3 seats remain and a call would put ≥ 50 % of the stack at risk, require `eq ≥ potOdds + 0.05·(placesAtRisk)`, a cheap ICM proxy.
- **B-6 · P1 · Power valuation is wrong for several keywords** (`powerValue`, `bot.ts:197-269`), and the keyword-impact run shows the result (HEAD: n = 4,833 resolved casts, 120 Quick 6-seat matches, `s3/head/kw.txt`; old scale n = 4,858 in brackets; hand Δ in BB for the caster, baseline −0.21 [−0.54]):
  - **Kindle** `:211` `nBB·(0.4+eq)` is positive at equity 0. Its real EV is `eq·(N+cost) − cost`. Measured: Kindle cards Δ +0.67 BB, win 42 % [−1.71, 38 %], so the card result is noise-level, but **Leader Kindle 48 (Sentinel) cast 130×, win 20.8 %, Δ −2.43 BB** [133×, 18.8 %, −2.72] holds on both scales: the bot drains chips into pots it then loses. **Fix:** `nBB·r.equity − (costBB)·(1−r.equity)`, plus 0.2 for the nerve hit.
  - **Insurance** `:221-222` values `0.5·nBB` whenever eq < 0.6, but it only pays if the seat loses a showdown **all-in**. Measured: cast 153×, win 20.9 %, Δ −2.86 BB [139×, 20 %, −3.45]; Swirling Ink Cloud (Insurance 128) is the most negative flagged card in Standard (3.3). **Fix:** value 0 unless `r.stackBB ≤ r.potBB` or the bot is facing an all-in.
  - **Siphon** `:217` `min(nBB, potBB)`. Real EV is `(1−eq)·(N − cost)`, which is **0 at every printed tier** (see 3.4). Measured Δ −0.92 BB, win 43 % (n = 30) [−3.28, 15 %, n = 39]: negative on both scales but small samples. **Fix:** use the real EV, so it is cast only with Resonant/Surge/Happy Hour.
  - **Peek/Mark/Reveal** `:206-209` use a flat `0.25·potBB + 0.4`, which fires every hand once the price (8 chips) is a tiny share of the pot late in the match. Mark resolved **1,147×** [1,157×] in 120 matches (see C-3).
  - **Leader spend** `:398-399` `value > 0.8` with Kindle 48 / Tax 48 (3 BB) at level 0 is always true, so the bot spends nerve on its first chance and is often tilted (+1 step) later. **Fix:** require `value ≥ 1.2` and `nerve ≥ cost + 2` unless `potBB ≥ 6`.
  - **Leader build** `:399` `s.nerve ≤ 4 && rng < 0.5` ignores value and pays 8–16 chips. Diver's build is **Pass** (every seat passes a hole card left): it scrambles its own hand for chips. Leader Pass is used 182×, Δ −0.52 BB [204×, −0.75]. **Fix:** build only if `powerValue(pseudo) ≥ chipCost/bb`, or if nerve ≤ 1.
- **B-7 · P2 · Exclusions can cost the bot its own made hand.** `chooseExtraCosts` `:333` always prefers flush, straight, trips… in that order, as long as they are attainable. It does not check whether that category is the bot's **current** best hand, so a bot holding a flush that pays a tier-4 cost names "flush" and can no longer win with it. **Fix:** drop `evaluate(hole+board).category` and every category above it from `prefer`, and order the rest by how unlikely they are.
- **B-8 · P2 · Personas are nearly cosmetic.** `engine.ts:322-341`. The bluff rate spans 0.05 (Tide/Light) to 0.23 (Ember/Gale) and is scaled by `(1−0.6·skill)` to 0.03–0.15. Tightness moves the raise threshold by only `(t−0.5)·0.2 = ±0.03` equity. Two Leaders (Avatar of the Abyss, Void Mother) share Shadow/Void and so get **identical** personas and legal pools. Personas also ignore the Leader's abilities. **Fix:** raise the tightness coefficient at `:452` from 0.2 to 0.5, scale bluff by `(1−0.3·skill)`, and add a third axis, `aggression` (raise-vs-call preference), from the minus ability's colour, e.g. Ember +0.15 and Light −0.1.
- **B-9 · P2 · Difficulty is one scalar.** Easy, Normal and Hard differ only in `skill`, which drives noise, reading depth and a few thresholds. **Fix:** make difficulty a bundle. Easy: noise ×1.3, no reading, calls `margin −0.2`, never casts hostile powers at the human first. Hard: B-1…B-5 on, `equityTrials` 400, opponent model (VPIP/aggression per seat counted from `m.history`).
- **B-10 · P3 · Response windows.** Every eligible seat runs a full MC read per window (`:539-540`), and a raise also opens a window. That is fine at 5 ms, but `tryCast(..., inWindow=true)` can only Snuff, and **no Snuff card exists in the pool** (C-1), so windows are only used for Ambush/Quick value casts. Revisit once Snuff ships.
- **B-12 · P1 · Hard bots over-fold to aggression.** In the table above, two trivial strategies (shove the top half pre-flop; bet ¾ pot whenever checked to) lose badly to Easy bots (40.5 % / 38.0 % placement on HEAD) but **break even against Hard** (47.5 % / 50.0 %), beating the Hard bot's own seat-0 result of 39.0 % (old scale: 44 / 32.5 vs Easy, 53 / 51.5 vs Hard). The cause is skill-scaled pessimism stacked twice: the pressure exponent `eq^(1 + 1.4·pressure·(0.3+0.7·skill))` (`bot.ts:458`), plus `rangeFloors` that read every bet as 0.3–0.6 strength (`:151-162`), with only 15 % `bluffMix` (`constants.ts:206`). The more skilled the bot, the more it folds. **Fix:** keep the range floor *or* the pressure exponent, not both (drop `:458` to `1 + 0.6·pressure` when `rangeFloors` is active). Raise `bluffMix` to 0.25 for opponents whose observed stab rate (count bets after checks in `m.history`) is above 40 %. Add a minimum-defence floor: facing a bet `b` into pot `P`, a Hard bot defends at least the top `P/(P+b)` of its range on the river.
- **B-11 · P3 · Free Peek always targets the threat** (`:544-547`); fine.

The proposed 3-min / 15-min Quick plays **+3.2 hands** (+23 %) with the cap deciding 18 % of tables instead of 28 %, at a cost of 1.8 nominal minutes. Pooled over both scales (n = 120 per row, SE ≈ 3.7 pts on win share, 2 pts on above-seat), the variants sit within 2 pts of each other on "above a seat" and within 7 pts on wins. **The old claim that slowing the clock lifts the Hard win rate to 28 % was noise**, so this finding is now P2 and rests on pace, not skill: 2.7 hands per level means a Quick player sees about 15 hands, one or two of them with a playable ★3+, before the cap ranks stacks. **Change:** Quick `levelMs 3 min / capMs 15 min` (about 4 hands per level), or express levels in **hands** (`handsPerLevel` 4 / 5 / 6 for Quick / Standard / Deep at 6 seats, ×1.5 heads-up), which also matches the OFF-timer design in 3.1.0. Pair it with F-8's 480-chip Quick stack if Quick should stay about 12 minutes.

#### F-2b · P3 · Blind rounding at the 50-chip boundary (`constants.ts:106-109`)

`roundBlind` switches from 2-chip to 10-chip steps at 50 and from 10 to 50 at 200, so the exact curve 36 → 54 → 81 lands on **36 → 50 → 80** (×1.39 then ×1.60) and 180 → 273 → 410 lands on **180 → 250 → 400** (same pair). The two steps cancel, so the level-to-level trend is intact and every blind is even, as intended. It is cosmetic, but a player sees the blinds jump 60 % once per four levels. **Change:** `const step = raw < 60 ? 2 : raw < 300 ? 10 : raw < 1000 ? 50 : 100;` gives 16 · 24 · 36 · **54** · 80 · 120 · 180 · **270** · 400 · 600 · 900 · 1,400: every step ×1.48–1.56. The whole-chip test (`engine.test.ts:409-418`, which only checks `bb/2` is an integer) still passes, since every value is even. The old-scale note about level 2 rounding 2.25 → 2.5 is fixed at HEAD (36 chips is exact).

#### F-3 · P3 · Time-cap tie-break favours seat 0, which is always the human (`engine.ts:1278`)

`sort((a,b) => b.stack − a.stack || a.idx − b.idx)`. On a stack tie at the cap, the human always places higher. **Change:** break ties by stack at the start of the final hand, then by the last pot won.

#### F-4 · OK · Pot-limit sizing

`betOptions` (`engine.ts:742-764`) gives `max raise-to = currentBet + pot + owe` and `min = currentBet + max(lastRaise, bb)`, clamped for Siphon/Blessed shrinking the pot. That is correct. Power costs land in the pot before betting, so casting first raises your own pot-limit cap. That is an intended "cast is a bet" interaction, but the rulebook doesn't say so (§2.5): add one line. The UI presets (`PokerTable.tsx:1155-1159`) and the bot `raiseTo` (`bot.ts:472-476`) use the same formula.

#### F-5 · P0 · Placement rewards pay more to concede than to play, and heads-up pays 2.5–3× per minute

`rewards.ts:24-33`; migration `poker_place_factor` and `record_match_placement`; `REWARD_BASE` `constants.ts:233`; concede `PokerTable.tsx:536-545` (reported place = number of seats still alive, i.e. **last place**).

Credits per real minute at 1× bot speed, with the HEAD lab's n = 20 match lengths (`s3/head/length20.txt`) and the expected place factor for an average player:

| Format | E[credits] | Real min | **Credits/min** | Concede at the server floor: credits / floor min = per min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quick 2 | 35 | 4.7 | **7.4** | 20 / 2 = **10.0** |
| Quick 6 | 30.8 | 12.2 | 2.5 | 20 / 6 = **3.3** |
| Standard 2 | 70 | 8.9 | **7.9** | 40 / 4 = **10.0** |
| Standard 4 | 65.5 | 15.8 | 4.1 | 40 / 8 = 5.0 |
| Standard 6 | 61.5 | 20.9 | 2.9 | 40 / 12 = **3.3** |
| Deep 6 | 92.3 | 32.9 | 2.8 | 60 / 20 = 3.0 |

Last place pays 40 % of a win (`REWARD_BASE.credits = [40, 60]`), so **sitting idle until the floor and then pressing CONCEDE out-earns playing** in every Standard/Quick format. Heads-up Standard concessions pay **600 credits and 375 XP per hour** under the 15/day full-rate taper (they count as "other places"), against about 175 credits per hour from playing 6-seat Standard. XP is the same story: 25 XP per 4 min against 1.8 XP/min playing 6-seat. The chip change made heads-up Quick 22 % shorter, so its credits per minute rose from 6.3 to 7.4.

**Changes:**
1. `REWARD_BASE.credits = [15, 85]`, xp `[10, 50]`, bpXp `[8, 42]` (the win still pays 100/60/50 at Standard, last place 15/10/8). Mirror it in the SQL.
2. A seat-count multiplier `{2: 0.55, 3: 0.7, 4: 0.8, 5: 0.9, 6: 1}`, which roughly equalises credits per minute across table sizes.
3. Concede pays 0 before `max(3 × seats, 8)` hands. Send `p_hands` (the client already has `m.handNo`) and clamp it server-side to `≤ floor_seconds / 15`.
4. Show the reward preview on the concede confirm.

#### F-6 · P2 · XP and level curve (`supabase/migrations/20261006000000_economy_rebalance_and_slab_showcase.sql:33-45`: `xp_for_level = 20·L·(L−1)`, `level_reward_credits = 75`, +10 vouchers per 5 levels)

Level L → L+1 costs 40·L XP. An average Standard 6-seat match pays 37.5 XP in 21 real min (HEAD). Level 10 → 11 takes about 11 matches (4 h), level 20 → 21 about 21 matches (8 h), level 50 → 51 about 53 matches (20 h). The reward per level is flat (75 credits), so the level reward per hour decays as 1/L. Reaching level 20 takes about 7,600 XP, roughly 200 Standard 6-seat matches or about 71 h. **Change:** `xp_for_level = round(60·(L−1)^1.6)` (level 20 at ≈ 6,700 XP, level 50 at ≈ 30,400, vs 7,600 and 49,000 today) and level reward `50 + 5·L` credits, capped at 300. `level_for_xp` (`:38-41`) is the closed-form inverse of today's quadratic, so it must be replaced in the same migration (a loop or `floor(1 + (xp/60)^(1/1.6))` with a ±1 correction), or levels and XP disagree. Coordinate with the economy pass.

#### F-7 · P2 · Rarity buys power (`cardpool.ts:114-127`, `RARITY_BIAS 0.1`)

Census (233 powers): mean tier **Common 2.22 · Uncommon 2.39 · Rare 2.50 · Super-Rare 3.11 · Full-Art 3.48**. Tier 4–5 share is **14 % Common vs 56 % Full-Art**. Rulebook §4 says rarity "gently biases the tier roll". Full-Art cards also roll 0–2 "chaos" modifiers (`cardpool.ts:232`). Tier is cost, not strength, so this is not pay-to-win by itself, but every ★5 slot (Quick allows one) goes to high-rarity cards. **Change:** `RARITY_BIAS 0.1 → 0.04`, or roll the tier from `seed` alone and let rarity pick art and modifiers only.

#### F-8 · P2 · Nerve is fine; the 25 % stack cap now bites in Quick (`constants.ts:130`, `engine.ts:1325-1329`)

Nerve starts at 5 with a cap of 10. With one Leader ability per hand and spend costs of 2–3, a Quick match of about 15 hands supports 4–5 spends. That is fine.

The stack cap is new trouble at HEAD. `cap = floor(stack × 0.25)`; when the chip cost is above it, the chips are clipped and **one more second cost** is added. A ★5 costs 96 chips, so the cap binds below **384 chips**. On the old scale (6 units against a 30-unit Quick stack) it bound below 80 % of the starting stack. At HEAD it binds below **96 %** of the 400-chip Quick stack: losing a single big blind turns the one ★5 a Quick deck may hold into a two-extra-cost card. A ★4 (56 chips) binds below 224 chips (56 % of start; was 47 %). Standard (750) and Deep (1,250) are unaffected in practice (★5 binds below 51 % / 31 % of start).

**Change** (pick one): set Quick `stackChips: 480` (30 BB, restoring the old depth, the old cap threshold and the old match length; see F-2), or make the cap `max(STACK_CAP_SHARE × stack, COST_LADDER_UNITS[5] × UNIT)` while the seat holds at least its starting stack. The first is simpler and also restores Quick's hand count.

Numbers at HEAD scale: a card at ★1–★5 costs 8 · 16 · 32 · 56 · 96 chips. The chip keywords go in `TIER_N` in units, which all land on whole chips: Trap `[0, ½, 1, 2, 3, 5]`, Boat Bonus `[0, ½, 1, 1½, 2, 3]`. Trap at ★1 returns its 8-chip cost only if the raiser loses to you, so it is a conditional, never-positive-alone modifier like Bulwark; Boat Bonus pays N from each other seat, so in a 3-way pot a ★3 returns 48 chips (24 × 2) for a 32-chip cost and breaks even only at P(win with a full house or better) ≥ ⅔. That makes it a cast-when-made card, which is the intent: the bot values it as `P(win ∧ boat)·N·k − cost`. Overbet's N is a pot multiplier, not chips. Last Stand's 10 BB threshold is 160 chips at level 0 and 1,200 at level 5. Rush and Stall move the match clock by one level (`mode.levelMs`) forward or back; under the "fixed 5 s" OFF-timer design they still work, since they edit `clockMs`, and under a literal hand-count clock they edit `handNo`'s level offset instead.

Each needs a `KEYWORD_SPECS` entry (`keywords.ts:133`), a `TIER_N` row where numbered (`constants.ts:156`), an `applyEffect` case (`engine.ts:1928`), a `MIN_TIER` entry (`cardpool.ts:134`), and a `powerValue` case (`bot.ts:205` switch). Adding them changes the hash picks of existing cards. Gate them to **new set ids**, or pin current cards with `overrides` before shipping.

#### 4.0 What brings a player back today

**The play loop has one shape.** `PlayScreen` (`src/App.tsx:183`) offers 3 formats (Quick, Standard, Deep), 2 to 6 seats and 3 bot difficulties (`CPU_DIFFICULTIES`, `src/meta/matchPrefs.ts:199`). You can play a random deck (`buildDeck`) or one of your saved decks. That is every option.

**Variety within a match:**
- the 17-rule Location bag (`src/game/poker/locations.ts`), with a two-hand forecast;
- 9 Leaders with two-colour identities;
- a fresh seed for each match (`newMatchSeed`).

The rest of the loop:
- **Rewards** are flat by finishing place (`src/game/poker/rewards.ts`). The deck you bring makes no difference to them.
- **REMATCH** (`PokerTable.tsx:1956`, `App.tsx:730`) rolls a brand-new table.

**CPU opponents are anonymous and interchangeable:**
- Each seat is named after its Leader.
- Its persona is computed from the Leader's colours (`personaFor`, `engine.ts:331`).
- Its deck is a fresh random `buildDeck` fill every match (`cpuTableSetup`, `sim.ts:24`).

So you never meet the same opponent twice, and nothing about a bot is worth learning.

**Most accounts cannot play at all.** `isCpuLocked` (`src/meta/cpuAccess.ts`) locks PLAY for every non-Creator account (the MainMenu tile shows COMING SOON!). Guests can play only the random-deck quick match. For an ordinary account, the loop that repeats today is entirely the collection economy:
- daily login and daily pack;
- weekly bingo, which is judged on the collection (`BingoPanel.tsx`);
- Card of the Day;
- packs, marketplace, player shops and grading;
- the battle pass, whose XP comes from matches those accounts cannot play.

Every design below assumes this gate is lifted. That is a product call, and this pass treats it as a precondition, not as a finding.

**Deck-building tools that exist:**
- legality checks with one-click fixes (`deck.ts checkDeck`, `deckEdits.ts`);
- the tier curve and top keywords;
- a DECK GUIDE: curve, type mix, colour and four themes (`deckAdvice.ts`);
- POWER HAND goldfish odds (`goldfish.ts`);
- quickbuild from owned cards;
- an undo stack;
- deck code and link import/export (`deckcode.ts`, `FRY2:<mode>:<leader>:<cards>`);
- duplicate deck;
- Card of the Day's "build with" hand-off.

The tools are good at *checking* a deck. They do little to help a player *discover* one.

#### 4.1 Where the loop runs dry

1. **The challenge never grows.** Difficulty is a toggle, not a path, so nothing marks progress from one session to the next. Match History (`matchHistory.ts`) is a local list of 50 matches with summary stats.
2. **There is no reason to build a second deck.**
   - Every saved deck plays in the same three formats against the same random tables.
   - The rewards ignore what you played.
   - Copies are locked to one deck at a time (`validateDeckList` `lockedByOtherDecks`, `DeckBuilderScreen.tsx:133`), so a second deck costs a second set of cards.
   - Once a player has a deck that places well, nothing pushes them to try anything else.
3. **The packs feed only the collection.** There is no limited format, so 120 Commons in a 297-card pool are mostly binder filler once a player owns better cards.
4. **There are no short, self-contained goals.** The shortest unit of play is a 12-minute Quick freezeout. Nothing exists on the 2–5 minute scale (a puzzle, a single spot).
5. **There is no shared reference point.** Players cannot compare results (no common seed, no shared table), and the only leaderboard ranks collections (`fetchCardsLeaderboard`).
6. **Opponents are not characters.** Nothing makes you want to beat a particular one.

#### 4.2 Designs

Effort: **S** is about 1 day or less, **M** is a few days to a week, **L** is more than a week or needs server work. "On roadmap" means `docs/ROADMAP.md` already names the idea.

The engine has one useful property for nearly everything below:
- `MatchSetup.seats[]` already carries a per-seat `name`, `deck` and `persona` (`engine.ts:309`, `sim.ts:24`).
- `modeOf(m)` (`engine.ts:344`) is the single funnel through which the engine reads its mode config.
- `replay(setup, actions)` (`engine.ts:2339`) rebuilds any point in a match.

Named opponents, mutators and puzzles can therefore mostly be built as **setup data**, not as engine rewrites.

##### R1. The Circuit: a CPU ladder with named opponents (**on roadmap** as "ELO-tracked CPU gauntlet") — M (L with a server rating)

**Opponents.** A roster of about 20 hand-authored opponents in a new data module, `src/game/poker/opponents.ts`. Each one is a `{ id, name, title, leaderId, deckCode, persona overrides, signature Location, intro/outro lines }`. Examples:
- "Salt-Tooth Maren": Mer-King, a Tide/Light Information deck, tight-passive.
- "The Cinder Twins": Ember, Bomb Pot, loose.

**Tiers.** Opponents are grouped into tiers (Bronze, Silver, Gold, Mythic). A tier's table is 3 to 4 named seats, plus a boss seat that has a hand-tuned deck and the highest skill.

**Rating.** A local rating first (localStorage next to `matchHistory.ts`), then a server column once placement becomes server-authoritative. The roadmap wants match history first, and the local history already exists.

**How it fits:**
- `cpuTableSetup` takes an optional `opponents: SeatSetup[]` and stops auto-filling those seats.
- Decks come from `decodeDeckCode`.
- Personas are `personaFor(leader, skill)` plus overrides.
- A PLAY › CIRCUIT sub-route goes into `routes.ts` (`play` sub `['circuit']`).

**Why it adds replay:**
- Opponents you can learn: a fixed deck and fixed tendencies.
- A goal gradient: a tier to clear and a boss to beat.
- A reason to tune a deck against a known field.

It is also the content backbone that R2, R3 and R11 reuse.

##### R2. Long Night: a roguelike gauntlet run — L

**The run.** A run is 5 to 7 short tables:
- heads-up or 3 seats;
- Quick blinds;
- you play a 16-power deck that **grows during the run**.

**Start.** Pick 1 of 3 Leaders, then take a fixed 8-card on-colour starter (`buildDeck` with `powers: 8`).

**Between tables:**
- Draft 1 of 3 cards. Use the slot rarity weights from `packodds.ts` so rarity feels like a pack.
- Pick 1 of 3 **boons**, for example:
  - "Your Location appears twice per cycle".
  - "Start each table at 7 nerve".
  - "Your Items never pay the unbonded step".
  - "+5 starting stack".
  - "See a third forecast Location".
- Some nodes are a curse-for-reward trade, for example "Opponents start with +10 stack; boon pick +1".

**Stacks** carry over, partly or fully (a run-health bar). Bust out and the run ends.

**How it fits:**
- **Engine:** `SeatSetup` gains an optional `stackUnits`, read in `createMatch` (`engine.ts:480`). Boons become `Match.mutators` that `modeOf` and the relevant rule points read (see R3).
- **Opponents:** taken from the R1 roster, scaling by node.
- **Run state:** a serialisable `{seed, node, deck, boons, stack}` in localStorage. A run can resume *between* tables, which avoids the mid-match resume problem `matchHistory.ts` documents.
- **Rewards:** paid once at run end by nodes cleared. This needs a run ticket (server), or the run can ship unrewarded first.

**Why it adds replay:** every run is a different deck. It uses the whole pool, including Commons. It makes short runs that still mean something, and it is the strongest "one more go" format a card game has.

##### R3. Weekly Table Rules (mutators) — M

**The rotation.** One to two mutators per ISO week, chosen by hashing the week key. This is the same no-server trick as `cardOfTheDay` (`cardOfTheDay.ts:19`, FNV over `utcDayKey`).

**Kinds of mutator:**

| Kind | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Table | "Happy Hour every hand"; "Fog bag" (Fog joins every cycle); "Pineapple Week"; "Double Board on every Plain Table" |
| Economy | "Deep stacks, Quick blinds"; "Antes from hand 1" |
| Nerve | "Start at 2 nerve"; "Tilt Zone is permanent" |
| Deck (enforced at the PLAY gate) | "No tier-4+"; "Units only"; "Events cost one step less" |

**How it fits:**
- Add `MatchSetup.mutators?: MutatorId[]`, copied onto `Match`.
- `modeOf(m)` merges numeric overrides (stack, `blindGrowth`, `levelMs`).
- The Location-bag mutators hook `buildCycle`'s `house` argument (`locations.ts` `buildCycle`, called from `engine.ts:530`) or force a rule id.
- Cost mutators plug into `castCost` (`engine.ts:1307`), which already handles step shifts (Happy Hour, tilt).
- Deck mutators are an extra predicate passed to `checkDeck` (see R6).
- A "THIS WEEK" banner on `PlayScreen`.

**Why it adds replay:** every Monday the familiar game feels different, and decks that are weak normally get their week. No new cards are needed: it is cheap "content" from rules that already exist. It is not on the roadmap. Bingo rotates weekly, but it rotates collection goals, not play.

##### R4. Spot of the Day: poker-and-power puzzles — M

A daily single decision. You see the table, your hole cards, your power hand, the Location and the action so far. You pick one action (fold / call / raise size / cast X at target Y / exclusion), then see the scored answer and the run-out.

**Authoring.** Run `scripts/simulate-poker.ts` with a "mine spots" flag. It records `(setup, actionsPrefix)` wherever a hard bot's equity-vs-cost margin (`BOT.castMargin`, `bot.ts`) was near zero, or where a cast or exclusion changed the showdown winner. A curator picks about 60 and commits them as JSON, e.g. `src/game/poker/puzzles.json`.

**Runtime and grading:**
- Runtime is `replay(setup, prefix)`.
- The answer is graded by Monte-Carlo equity using the bot's own estimator over N run-outs.
- Rotation uses `utcDayKey`.
- A streak count is the only meta (local).

**Why it adds replay:** a 60-second daily habit. It teaches the depth that is unique to FryCards (casting as a bluff, exclusions, Location rules), which a 12-minute freezeout rarely isolates. It also works for guests: it is pure client-side and earns no reward.

##### R5. Sealed and Draft (limited formats on the pack system) — Sealed M, Draft L

**Sealed:**
- Open 6 **virtual** packs. They are rolled client-side from a real `pack_types.slot_config` with `packodds.ts` weights, seeded, and not granted to the collection.
- Pick a Leader from 3 offered, plus every Location you opened.
- Build a Quick deck (16 powers) in the normal `DeckEditor`, using the sealed pool in place of `availableQty`.
- Play a 3-match series against R1 opponents, or against bots whose decks are built from their own sealed pools.

**Draft:**
- 3 rounds of 8-card packs, pick 1 and pass, against 5 bot drafters.
- A bot picks by a simple score: colour fit to its first two picks, then tier and theme deficit, reusing `deriveDeckAdvice` themes.
- The same bots then play you at a 6-seat table with the decks they drafted.

**How it fits:**
- `DeckEditor` already separates the pool (owned minus locked) from legality, so a `poolOverride` prop is a contained change.
- `buildDeck`'s candidate list can take a pool argument for the bots.
- Ruling: decks may go 1 colour outside the Leader's identity? **No.** Keep `isColorLegal`. Leader choice is the first pick instead.

**Rewards / entry (server):** an entry fee in credits or a pack voucher, and a prize by series wins. Optionally "keep 1 card from your pool", which needs a `grant` RPC. That part is L. Ship unrewarded practice first (M).

**Why it adds replay:**
- No two events alike.
- Commons matter.
- It rewards knowing the whole 297-card pool, not owning it.
- It gives packs a use beyond the binder.

This is the classic long-tail format for collectible card games. It is not on the roadmap.

##### R6. Constructed formats beyond length (Singleton, Pauper, Monochrome, Low Stakes, Showcase) — M

A `FormatId` that sits **alongside** `ModeId`. Mode stays the match length; the format adds deck restrictions:

| Format | Restriction |
| --- | --- |
| Singleton | `maxCopies` 1 |
| Pauper | Common and Uncommon only. The pool has 120 Commons. |
| Monochrome | Only one of the Leader's two colours, plus colourless |
| Low Stakes | No tier 4–5, which removes second costs entirely |
| Showcase | Players Showcase 2026 cards plus colourless, once the set clears its 100-card bar (**the set is on roadmap**; the format is not) |

**How it fits:**
- `checkDeck(leaderId, ids, mode, format?)` (`deck.ts`) gains a predicate list.
- `deckcode.ts` gains an optional fifth field (`FRY2:<mode>:<leader>:<cards>:<format>`). Old codes decode as "open".
- The DeckBuilder format picker sits next to the mode tabs. `PlayScreen` filters decks by format.
- `buildDeck` filters its `candidates` by the same predicate, so bots play the format too.

**Check before shipping:** whether each Leader can legally fill 16, 24 and 36 powers under each restriction.
- **Pool shape:** some colour slices are thin. Light has 3 Events and Root has 2 Items.
- **Testing:** a unit test that runs `buildDeck` per Leader × format catches the impossible combinations.

**Why it adds replay:** it multiplies decks worth building out of the same collection, gives Commons and Uncommons a format, and pairs with R3 ("Pauper Week").

##### R7. Rewards that pay for off-meta choices — S to M

**Featured Leader and Location of the week.** A hash of the week, like R3, picks them. If your deck uses them, your placement credits get a bonus multiplier.

**Fresh Seat.** A small bonus when you play a Leader you have not played in your last N matches (read from `matchHistory`, `humanDeck`).

**Budget Deck.** A bonus when a deck's rarity score is below a threshold.

**How it fits:**
- `placementReward` (`rewards.ts`) gains a `bonuses` argument, and the game-over preview shows the line.
- The server's `record_match_placement` must mirror it, since the ticket records mode and seats. The ticket would also record the leader id and deck code.

These are **reward modifiers on a match**, not missions or achievements, so they stay in this lane.

**Why it adds replay:** players rotate their decks themselves, the Leaders that see the least play get attention, and nobody has to touch a card's numbers.

##### R8. Discovery tools in the builder: synergy, Location fit and gap-fillers — M

**Location fit.** A rule table from `LocationRuleId` to a deck property:
- Night Game against a deck heavy in Quick Events;
- Silent Table against Ambush carriers;
- Happy Hour as a reason to run tier 4–5;
- Tilt Zone against Leader abilities that spend nerve;
- Pineapple and Open Hand alongside Information themes.

The DECK GUIDE then says whether your own Location helps or hurts your powers. Your Location joins every cycle, so this matters.

**Leader fit.** The same idea for the two `abilities` on the Leader card.

**Gap-fillers.** For each theme or curve deficit `deriveDeckAdvice` reports, list the 3 best *owned, available* cards that fix it. Add a "show unowned" toggle; unowned cards get a ♥ wishlist button (`wishlist.ts toggleWishlisted` / `pushWishlistToggle`).

**Archetype label.** Generate a name from Leader colours plus the dominant theme (e.g. "Tide/Light Information").

**How it fits:** all pure functions added to `deckAdvice.ts` (already unit-tested), rendered in the existing DECK GUIDE panel (`DeckBuilderScreen.tsx:1008`). The roadmap's "Deck archetype guidance in the builder" is marked **[retired with the MTG-style game]**. This is its poker-era successor, so call it *on roadmap in spirit, not as written*.

**Why it adds replay:** it turns the builder from a checker into an idea generator, and that is where off-meta decks come from.

##### R9. Proxy and playtest decks — S to M

Allow saving and playing decks that use cards you **do not own**, or cards already locked in another deck. Such a deck:
- is flagged **PLAYTEST**;
- is always unrewarded (no ticket minted);
- cannot be entered in rewarded queues.

Ownership is already a separate layer: `validateDeckList` adds `ownership` issues *on top of* `checkDeck`. So the only changes are a playtest save path (a localStorage deck list, so the `save_deck` RPC is untouched) and an unrewarded branch in `Game` (`App.tsx` already skips the ticket for guests).

**Why it adds replay:** players can theorycraft a deck before buying into it. It feeds the marketplace and wishlist (a "missing cards" list). It also removes the copy-lock cost of experimenting.

##### R10. Practice table / sandbox — M

A table built to test a deck, not to play a freezeout:
- Heads-up against one passive bot (`powerUse` 0, `skill` low) or a mirror of your own deck.
- Pick a fixed Location (a forced bag).
- Infinite clock.
- "Deal again" (a new hand on the same stacks).
- An optional reveal-all mode for learning.

**How it fits:**
- `cpuTableSetup` with a `seat1` override.
- A mutator (R3) that empties the cap and the blind clock.
- `view.ts` already redacts per seat, and a debug "omniscient" view is one flag.

**Why it adds replay:** it closes the build → test → tweak loop that the goldfish odds (`goldfish.ts`) only approximate.

##### R11. Ghost decks: play against friends' and community decks (**PvP itself is on roadmap**; this is the async step before it) — S to M

Paste any deck code, or open a friend's shared link (`DeckLinkPreview.tsx`). Choose **"PLAY AGAINST THIS"**, and the deck takes a CPU seat under the friend's username, using the persona for its Leader.

A "Deck of the Week" boss slot, picked by the Creator or from Showcase cards, gives everyone the same target.

**How it fits:** `cpuTableSetup` seat override plus `deckDefFromCustom(leaderId, cardIds)`. No server is needed for the casual version. A per-week board of "best place against the boss" needs one small table.

**Why it adds replay:** decks become social objects. Sharing a code becomes a challenge, not just information. It gives a PvP-flavoured loop while PvP waits on server-authoritative matches.

##### R12. Daily Table: same seed for everyone — S (client and share text) / M (board)

One button, **DAILY TABLE**:
- The seed is `hash(utcDayKey)`.
- The format and seats are fixed.
- The human gets a pre-rolled random deck, so everyone starts from identical seats, decks, button and first deal. `buildTable` is already fully seed-driven (`PokerMatch.tsx:31`).
- Results produce a Wordle-style share line: "FryCards Daily 2026-10-10 · 2nd/6 · 31 hands · ◎84".
- A server board is optional.

**Rewards:** treat it as unrewarded, or once per day. A known seed leaks the first deal on a retry, so only the first attempt counts.

**Why it adds replay:** a daily appointment that players can compare, with almost no build cost.

##### R13. Leader trials: a curated campaign per Leader — M

Three authored scenarios per Leader (27 total), each a hand-built `MatchSetup`:
- named opponents (R1);
- mutators (R3);
- sometimes a fixed starting deck.

Each one shows off the kit. For example, Kuro the Unseen at a Fog-and-Open-Table table against two information decks.

Completion is shown as stars on the `LeaderPicker`. This is a single-player content mode, not a counter-based achievement. Rewards, if any, are one-time per trial.

**Why it adds replay:**
- It onboards each Leader's identity.
- It gives a finite, completable goal.
- It is the natural place to introduce the unprinted keywords once they print (on roadmap as `UNPRINTED_KEYWORDS`).

#### 4.3 Suggested sequencing

1. **Lift `isCpuLocked`, or widen it to Quick format, for accounts.** Without this, none of the above reaches the players who own collections.
2. **The S-sized layer:** R12, R11, R9, R7, plus §6. These share the "setup is data" seam and ship independently.
3. **R3, then R6.** R3 introduces the `mutators` and format-predicate seams that R1, R2, R5, R10 and R13 reuse.
4. **R1 (roadmap item), then R4.** The roster and the puzzle corpus are content work that can run in parallel with the code.
5. **R5 Sealed, then R2, then R5 Draft and R13.** These are the L-sized formats, built on all of the above.

#### Live schemas

There is no unique index on `name`, no `hidden` or `active` flag, and no rotation column.

#### How progress works

- `track_stat(uid, key, amount, absolute)` is SECURITY DEFINER and not executable by `anon` or `authenticated`. It upserts every `achievements` row and every `missions` row with that `stat_key`. Progress is capped at the target. An absolute stat keeps `greatest(old, new)`. A mission resets when `mission_period_start(cadence)` changes: the UTC day for daily, `date_trunc('week')` (Monday) for weekly.
- `get_missions()` returns **every** mission row. There is no rotation, so all 11 dailies and all 17 weeklies are live at the same time.
- `claim_mission` pays credits and vouchers, plus BP XP through `grant_bp_xp`. When no season is active, `grant_bp_xp` returns false and pays nothing. `claim_achievement` also grants `reward_pack_id`.

#### Economy reference (live)

- **Match payout:** `round((40 + 60·placeFactor)·mult)`, where mult is quick ×0.5, standard ×1, deep ×1.5. A Standard win pays 100 cr, a Deep 6-seat win 150 cr, and last place 20–60 cr.
- **Taper and cap:** 15 full-rate results per outcome per 24 h, then 25%. Hard cap of 100 results per 24 h.
- **Booster:** 599 cr or 6 vouchers, so **1 voucher ≈ 100 cr**. A Booster Box is 3,799 cr.
- **Daily login:** 250–1,000 cr plus 5 cr per streak day, a free pack on day 5 and 5 vouchers on day 7. That averages about 560 cr a day.
- **Bingo:** a line pays 120 cr. A blackout pays 750 cr + 5 vouchers.
- **Battle pass:** 25 tiers × `BP_XP_PER_TIER = 100` (src/lib/supabase.ts:700) = 2,500 XP. Season 1 runs 2026-07-12 to 2026-11-10.
- **Players:** 4 total, 1 creator. **0 poker match tickets have ever been redeemed.**

### (a) Problems with the current set

1. **Match objectives cannot be done the honest way, but can be farmed.** CPU play is locked client-side for every non-creator account (`src/meta/cpuAccess.ts:7-12`, greyed by `isMatchObjective` at `AchievementsScreen.tsx:41`). That makes 6 missions (d_play_3, d_win_1, d_win_2, w_games_10, w_play_15, w_win_8) and 9 battle achievements impossible through the UI. The server, however, has **no role check**:
   - `begin_match()` accepts any authenticated user, up to 60 tickets an hour.
   - The legacy `record_match_result(p_won, p_match_id)` is still `EXECUTE`-granted to `authenticated` with a **45 s** floor, and pays a 100 cr "win" with no mode or seats.
   - So a locked player with devtools can claim 15 full-rate "wins" a day without playing. That alone completes d_win_1, d_win_2 and w_win_8 (≈ 700 cr + 5 v + 300 BP XP a day/week), and progresses the battle achievements, which are greyed but still claimable through the RPC.

   **Fix (backend):** revoke `record_match_result` from `authenticated`. The client only uses it as a fallback when `record_match_placement` is missing, which it no longer is (src/lib/supabase.ts:597-603). Mirror the client lock in `begin_match`, or pay match stats only for `role = 'creator'` until CPU play opens.
2. **New poker objectives would not be greyed.** `MATCH_STAT_KEYS` (AchievementsScreen.tsx:37) holds only `games_played` and `wins`. Missions have no `category`, so every new match key in (b) must be added to that set, or locked players will see un-greyed missions they cannot finish.
3. **Retired twins are still visible and still accrue progress.** `win_100` ("Legend of Fry (Retired)", 0 cr; 1 holder, unclaimed, progress 1) and `collector_150` ("Completionist (Retired)", 0 cr; 4 holders) are fetched by `fetchAchievements` (`select('*').order('sort')`, supabase.ts:777) and listed with a 0-credit reward. The schema has no hidden flag.
   - **Fix:** delete `win_100`. Its only holder sits at 1/100 and `wins_100` covers it, so nothing is lost.
   - For `collector_150`, add `hidden bool` with a client filter, or delete it. Its claim record goes with it, but it paid 0.
4. **Alt-farmable keys pay missions today.**
   - `listings_created` counts listings that end **expired**. A 1-hour auction of 1 card costs nothing (no listing fee; the card comes back), and 10 may be active, so that is about 240 a day. It pays d_list_1 and listings_25.
   - `friends` is +1 for both sides on accept and is never decremented. `remove_friend` followed by a re-add loops it with one alt. It pays friend_1/5 and friends_10.
   - `trades` is +1 for both sides. Two alts can trade 1 credit back and forth (`create_trade` only refuses an empty trade). It pays trade_1, trades_25 and w_trade_1 (400 cr + 2 v a week).
   - `market_sales` / `market_buys`: a 1 cr fixed listing bought by an alt costs `ceil(1×5%) = 1` cr and ticks +1 for both. It pays w_market_1 (**400 cr + 2 v for one sale**), market_sale_1, market_buy_1, market_sales_25 and market_buys_25.
   - `shop_sales`: an alt buys from your player shop and the credits recycle. It pays shop_sales_10.
   - **Fix (emitters):** count a market sale, buy or shop sale only at ≥ 25 cr, and never between friends. Count `trades` once per pair per day, only with ≥ 1 card moving. Make `friends` once per pair, ever (`stat_once 'friend:'||least(a,b)||greatest(a,b)`). Count `listings_created` only on `sold`. Until then, (b) adds **no new row** on these keys. The SQL keeps them in a held-back section.
5. **`decks_built` daily = a free daily payout.** A delete-and-re-save of the same list mints a new deck id, and `stat_once 'deckday:'` allows one a day. The existing w_deck_1 (250 cr a week) is acceptable. A daily version is not, so the draft's `d_deck_1` was dropped.
6. **Battle-pass XP from missions is bigger than the pass.** Weekly missions give 2,840 XP and dailies 385 × 7 = 2,695 XP, against 2,500 XP for all 25 tiers. A market-active player finishes the pass in under 4 days. Also, Season 1 ends 2026-11-10 and no Season 2 row exists; after that every mission's BP XP silently pays 0. **Fix:** halve weekly BP XP, cut daily by a third (optional SQL §0), and seed Season 2.
7. **No rotation.** `get_missions` returns every row, so each new daily is a permanent addition to daily income. With the ≥ 15 new dailies below, the daily list would be about 30 rows. **Fix (backend + client):** add `missions.pool text` (or `weight`), and have `get_missions` / `track_stat` / `claim_mission` use only the N rows picked by `hashtext(id || period_start) % k`. Keep `d_login`, `d_pack_1` and `d_win_1` always on, and rotate 4 more each day and 5 more each week. Until rotation exists, the SQL adds **only 4 dailies and 3 weeklies** on existing keys.
8. **New tiers on +1 keys start from zero.** `track_stat` inserts a missing `player_achievements` row with `progress = amount` (1), not lifetime totals. Without a backfill, a veteran's new `wins_500` would read 1/500. The SQL backfills new rows from the highest progress any sibling row with the same key already holds, and from source tables where one exists (`profiles.wins`, `profiles.games_played`, `graded_cards`).
9. **Unreachable collection targets: the Leader supply.**

#### c.2 New stat keys and where to emit them

**Match keys.** These come from the engine, are tallied client-side and are paid server-side. The single transport works like this:

1. `Match` (engine.ts:259) gets `tally: SeatTally[]`, one entry per seat, with counters plus `kw: string[]`, `minStackBB: number` and `bigPots`. `createMatch` (engine.ts:479) initialises it.
2. `PokerTable.tsx:527` passes `stats: m.tally[HUMAN]` and `leaderId: m.seats[HUMAN].leader.id` into `onResult`.
3. `App.tsx:454/463` forwards them to `recordMatchPlacement(…, stats)` (src/lib/supabase.ts:588), which sends them as `p_stats jsonb`.
4. `record_match_placement` gets a new argument, `p_stats jsonb default null`. Old clients keep working. Inside it, and **only after the ticket UPDATE succeeds and only when `not v_reduced`**, each key is clamped to a per-match ceiling, then `track_stat` runs.
   - Per-match ceilings by mode (quick / standard / deep): hands-type keys ≤ 40 / 80 / 120; `made_*` ≤ 6; `made_quads` and `made_straight_flush` ≤ 1; `seats_busted` ≤ `p_seats − 1`; `triple_bust` ≤ 1 and only when `p_seats ≥ 4`; `big_pots` ≤ 5.
   - The same mitigations as `wins` apply: the server ticket, its length floor, the taper and the daily cap. Values stay client-asserted until matches are server-authoritative, so per-match clamps keep the worst case at "max per legitimate match".

### 1. Recommendations

Priorities: **P1** = do now (large or compounding saving, or a live bug). **P2** = next pass. **P3** = hygiene.

#### S1 (P1). Move the always-on per-user subscriptions off `postgres_changes` (the wal2json poller)

**Evidence**
- 69.7 % of all DB execution time is the Realtime poller: 390 k calls at 5.85 ms.
- It runs whenever **any** `postgres_changes` subscription exists. `MetaContext.tsx:424-432` opens 3 of them (profiles, player_cards, player_inventory) for **every signed-in user for the whole session**, so the poller never idles while anyone is online.
- Each change on a subscribed table is also RLS-checked once per subscriber.
- A Box open writes up to 49 `player_cards` rows, which means 49 decoded changes, 49 RLS evaluations and 49 websocket messages. The client then debounces all of them into a single refetch (350 ms, `MetaContext.tsx:418-422`). The per-row payload is thrown away.

**Change**, any one of these (best first):
1. **Database Broadcast instead of postgres_changes.** Add `AFTER INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE … FOR EACH STATEMENT` triggers on `profiles`, `player_cards`, `player_inventory` that call `realtime.send(jsonb_build_object('t', TG_TABLE_NAME), 'changed', 'user:' || uid, true)`, with one message per statement, not per row. Use a private channel and an RLS policy on `realtime.messages` that lets a user read only topic `user:<own uid>`. Broadcast goes through the pgoutput `supabase_realtime_messages_publication` slot (streaming, already active) instead of the polled wal2json slot.
   ```sql
   create or replace function public.notify_user_change() returns trigger
   language plpgsql security definer set search_path = public as $$
   begin
     perform realtime.send(jsonb_build_object('table', TG_TABLE_NAME), 'changed',
                           'user:' || (select distinct user_id from new_rows limit 1), true);
     return null;
   end $$;
   -- one per table, statement-level with transition table:
   create trigger player_cards_notify after insert or update on public.player_cards
     referencing new table as new_rows for each statement execute function public.notify_user_change();
   ```
   Client: replace `subscribeTable(...)` in `MetaContext.tsx:424-432` with one `supabase.channel('user:'+userId, {config:{private:true}}).on('broadcast',{event:'changed'}, …)` that dispatches to the refreshers by `payload.table`.
2. **Minimum version: one subscription instead of three.** Keep only `profiles`. Have every RPC that changes another user's cards or inventory (trade accept, shop sale, auction settle, admin grant) also bump `profiles.updated_at`. Most already do, for example `settle_expired_listings`. On a profile event, refresh profile, collection and inventory together.
3. Then remove from the publication any table nobody subscribes to: `ALTER PUBLICATION supabase_realtime DROP TABLE public.decks;` (no client subscriber; grep shows none).

**Estimated savings**
- Option 1 removes the wal2json poller whenever only those three are subscribed. That is about **70 % of DB CPU at idle**: about 23 s/day today, and it scales with online minutes, not with players.
- Per Box open: 49 → 1 realtime message.
- Option 2 cuts per-user subscriptions 3 → 1 and per-pack-open decoded events from 8–49 down to 1.

#### S2 (P1). Stop double-fetching after every purchase, open and claim

**Evidence**
- `StoreScreen.tsx` calls `refreshProfile(); refreshCollection(); refreshInventory();` explicitly after each RPC (lines 215-216, 246-247, 275-277, 300, 333-335, 367-369, 394-395, 415, 1268, 1289). There are 62 such call sites app-wide.
- The realtime subscription from S1 then fires 350 ms later and runs the same refreshers again.
- `refreshCollection` is 2 requests (`player_cards` plus `fetchMySerializedCards`), and the latter is itself 2 (`player_serialized_cards` plus `serialized_supply`; `supabase.ts:2128-2135`). Booster open = 1 RPC + **about 10 GETs** (5 explicit + 5 realtime-driven).
- pg_stat_statements agrees: `profiles?id=eq` about 811 calls, `player_cards` 588, `player_serialized_cards` 518, `serialized_supply` 491, against only 102 `open_pack`, 37 `claim_daily_login` and 25 `claim_daily_pack` calls.

**Change**
- (a) Have the write RPCs return the post-state the client needs. They already return pulls; add `credits`, `vouchers` and `xp`. Patch MetaContext state locally (`setProfile(p => ({...p, credits}))`) and drop the explicit `refreshProfile()` calls.
- (b) Pick one mechanism. Either remove explicit refreshes where the realtime/broadcast event will arrive, or (if S1 option 2) keep explicit refreshes for own actions and ignore events within N ms of a local RPC.
- (c) Cache `serialized_supply` (3 static rows) with `cachedFetch('serialSupply', 24h, …)` in `supabase.ts:2134`. It is refetched on every collection refresh today (491 calls for a 3-row constant table).

**Estimated savings:** about **5–7 of 11 requests per pack open, claim or purchase (−50–65 %)**. `serialized_supply` traffic drops about 100 %.

#### S3 (P1). Batch bulk quicksell

**Evidence**
- `quicksell_cards` is the most-called RPC: 567 calls, 1.5 s total. The second most-called is `get_card_market_value` (327).
- `CollectionScreen.tsx:97-120` ("sell all spare <rarity>") and `PackOpening.tsx:967-990` loop over cards, making **one RPC per (card, foil) pair, sequentially**.
- Each call changes `player_cards` and `profiles`, so with the realtime debounce at 350 ms and per-RPC latency of about 150–300 ms, many iterations also trigger a full profile+collection refetch.
- Selling 60 distinct commons is 60 RPCs, plus up to about 60 × 4 GETs in the worst case.

**Change:** add `quicksell_many(p_items jsonb)`, taking `[{card_id, qty, foil}]`. Use one transaction, one `track_stat` call and one profile update, and return `{sold, total, per_item[]}`. Replace both loops with one call.

**Estimated savings:** N → 1 RPC per bulk sell (for example 60 → 1), and the same reduction in realtime events, WAL and RLS work.

#### S4 (P2). Index the pack-draw query (pack opens are the heaviest app RPC)

**Evidence**
- `open_pack` averages **69.6 ms** and about **3,900 shared buffers per call** (102 calls, 397 k buffers).
- `random_card_of_rarity` runs `SELECT id FROM cards WHERE rarity=$1 AND card_type<>'Leader' [AND set_name = ANY($2)] ORDER BY random() LIMIT 1` once per card drawn.
- EXPLAIN shows **Seq Scan on cards** (72 pages: 576 kB heap for 297 rows, because each row carries the `template` jsonb and text columns) plus a Sort. A Box costs about 49 × 72 = 3.5 k page reads.
- `cards` shows seq_scan 1,550 and seq_tup_read 428 k.

**Change**
```sql
create index concurrently cards_draw_idx on public.cards (rarity, set_name) include (id)
  where card_type <> 'Leader';
-- leader draws:
create index concurrently cards_leader_draw_idx on public.cards (rarity, set_name) include (id)
  where card_type = 'Leader';
vacuum (analyze) public.cards;  -- 96 dead tuples; lets the index-only scan skip the heap
```
For a further step, cache the per-rarity id arrays in a tiny `card_draw_pool(rarity, set_name, ids text[])` table, refreshed by `apply_card_upsert`, and pick `ids[1 + floor(random()*cardinality(ids))]`.

**Estimated savings:** about 90 % fewer buffer reads per draw (72 → about 2–3 pages). `open_pack` and `buy_and_open_packs` drop from about 70 ms to about 10–15 ms.

#### S5 (P2). Main-menu badge fetch runs 3 requests on every visit to the menu

**Evidence**
- `MainMenu.tsx:259-276` fetches `get_missions` + `achievements` (the 63-row static catalog) + `player_achievements` every time MainMenu mounts.
- These are the three most frequent endpoints in the last 24 h: 16–17 each, versus 5–11 for everything else.

**Change**

- Compute the badge from one RPC `get_claimable_count()`, or return it from `get_missions`. Re-run it only when `profile.updated_at` changes, not on every mount.

**Estimated savings:** 3 → 1 request per menu visit (−66 %), and the catalog drops to about 0.

#### S6 (P2). Card catalog: lengthen the cache and make refresh conditional

**Evidence**

- 264 catalog fetches since July (159 authenticated + 105 anon). The PostgREST query is 17.5 ms mean.

**Change**, either of:

**Estimated savings:** about 97 kB × (4 loads/day/player) becomes about 0.4 kB/day/player. That is about 99 % of catalog egress and all catalog DB reads.

#### S7 (P2). Tighten the unfiltered realtime subscriptions on secondary screens

#### S9 (P3). Cron hygiene

**Evidence**

#### S10 (P3). Drop backup tables, redundant indexes and unindexed FKs

**Backup tables:** `cards_art_backup_20261006` (264 kB), `cards_mechanics_backup_20261009` (136 kB), `cards_mechanics_backup_20261010` (72 kB) and `pack_types_art_backup_20261006` (16 kB) total about 490 kB, which is 14 % of the public schema.   - `cards_art_backup_20261006`, `cards_mechanics_backup_20261010` and `pack_types_art_backup_20261006` still carry full default grants to anon and authenticated (RLS blocks them, but revoke or drop anyway).

**Redundant indexes:** `player_cards_user_idx (user_id)` duplicates the leading column of `player_cards_pkey (user_id, card_id)`. `shop_slots_owner_idx (owner)` duplicates `shop_slots_owner_slot_index_key (owner, slot_index)`. Drop both: this saves write work on every `player_cards` upsert (1,001 updates so far).
  - The advisor's other 39 "unused" indexes are FK-covering or on empty shop tables. Keep them, because the shop feature is simply unused yet.

**Unindexed FKs (advisor):** `graded_cards.card_id`, `player_wishlist.card_id`, `shop_customers.listing_id`, `shop_customers.offer_card_id`. These matter only when a `cards`/`shop_listings` row is deleted. Add them if Creator card deletes are expected.

Several of these policies also target role `public`. Use `to authenticated` so anon requests skip them entirely.
